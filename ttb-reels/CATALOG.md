### P01-01

**Built before farming existed** · S1 Cold Case · Megaliths · KNOWN · ~42 s (97 words)

1. This temple in southern Turkey is around eleven and a half thousand years old.
2. That's older than Stonehenge by six thousand years. Older than the pyramids by seven.
3. But the strange part isn't the age. It's who built it.
4. No pottery. No farms. No metal tools. The people who carved these five-meter pillars were hunter-gatherers.
5. Archaeologists used to think you needed farming first, then cities, then temples.
6. Göbekli Tepe flipped that. The temple came first. Some researchers think feeding the builders helped push people toward farming.
7. Religion may not have followed civilization. It may have started it.

### P01-02

**A 2,000-year-old computer** · S1 Cold Case · Ancient technology · KNOWN · ~47 s (112 words)

1. In 1901, sponge divers off a Greek island pulled up a lump of corroded bronze from a Roman-era shipwreck.
2. For decades nobody understood it. Then X-rays and CT scans looked inside.
3. There were gears. At least thirty of them, with triangular teeth cut by hand.
4. Turn a crank, and it showed where the Sun and Moon were in the sky, the Moon's phase, and when eclipses would happen, years in advance.
5. It even tracked the four-year cycle of the Olympic Games.
6. Nothing with gearing this complex shows up again for over a thousand years, until medieval astronomical clocks.
7. The Greeks didn't just watch the sky. They built a machine to hold it.

### P01-03

**Roman glass that changes color** · S2 One Image · Ancient technology · KNOWN · ~45 s (106 words)

1. This is the Lycurgus Cup, made in Rome around sixteen hundred years ago.
2. Light it from the front, and it's a dull jade green.
3. Put a light inside it, and it glows blood red.
4. For centuries nobody knew why. In 1990, scientists looked at the glass under an electron microscope.
5. They found particles of gold and silver, about fifty to a hundred nanometers wide, a thousand times thinner than a hair.
6. Those particles scatter light one way and let it through another. Today we call that nanotechnology.
7. The Romans almost certainly didn't understand it. But they made it work, probably by accident, and kept the recipe.

### P01-04

**The ancient battery that isn't** · S4 Myth vs Record · Ancient tech myths · DEBATED · ~49 s (117 words)

1. You've probably seen it. The Baghdad Battery, proof that ancient people had electricity.
2. Here's what it actually is. A clay jar, a copper cylinder, an iron rod. Found near Baghdad and described in 1938.
3. Fill it with vinegar, and yes, you get about half a volt. People have tested it.
4. But nothing else fits. No wires were ever found. No other battery parts. Nothing it could have powered.
5. And the iron rod was sealed inside with asphalt, which makes it hard to connect to anything.
6. Most archaeologists think it held rolled scrolls, like similar jars from the region, and the metal simply corroded.
7. It can make electricity. There's just no sign anyone ever used it that way.

### P01-06

**Why an hour has 60 minutes** · S10 The Thread · Sumer & Mesopotamia · KNOWN · ~46 s (108 words)

1. Sixty seconds, sixty minutes. Why sixty, when we count everything else in tens?
2. Blame Mesopotamia. Over four thousand years ago, Sumerian and later Babylonian scribes counted in base sixty.
3. Sixty is great for trade. It divides cleanly by two, three, four, five, six, ten, twelve, fifteen, twenty and thirty.
4. Babylonian astronomers used it to track the sky. Greek astronomers like Ptolemy copied their system.
5. So when the hour was later cut into smaller pieces, it was cut the Babylonian way.
6. Same with the circle. Three hundred and sixty degrees. Six times sixty.
7. Every time you check the time, you're using math invented by people who wrote on clay.

**Pythagoras was 1,000 years late** · S2 One Image · Ancient mathematics · DEBATED · ~46 s (109 words)

1. This is a clay tablet called Plimpton 322. It's about thirty-eight hundred years old.

### P01-09

**Roman concrete heals itself** · S8 Made Simple · Ancient technology · KNOWN · ~47 s (112 words)

1. Modern concrete cracks within decades. Roman harbors have stood in seawater for two thousand years.
2. For a long time the answer was volcanic ash. That's part of it.
3. But in 2023, an MIT team found something else. Tiny white chunks of lime scattered through the concrete.
4. Everyone assumed those were mistakes, bad mixing. They weren't.
5. The Romans likely mixed in hot quicklime. When a crack forms and water gets in, those lime chunks dissolve and refill the crack with fresh mineral.
6. The team made their own version, cracked it, ran water through, and in two weeks the cracks had sealed.
7. The Pantheon's dome is still the largest unreinforced concrete dome on Earth.

### P02-02

**Scientists hid images for the dying to find** · S4 Myth vs Record · Clinical NDE studies · KNOWN · ~49 s (116 words)

1. If people really float above their bodies when the heart stops, you could test it. So researchers did.
2. The AWARE studies, led by Sam Parnia, hid pictures in hospital rooms, visible only from near the ceiling.

7. And brain recordings showed near-normal activity coming back up to an hour into CPR. Not proof of a soul. But not a dark, empty brain either.

### P02-03

**What Egyptians had to say to get into the afterlife** · S5 Voice from the Past · Ancient texts on death · KNOWN · ~52 s (123 words)

1. Ancient Egyptians believed that after death you faced a trial. And you had to give a speech.
